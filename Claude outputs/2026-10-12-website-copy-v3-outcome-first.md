## The rule applied to every line
- **Headline = the result the reader gets**, in their words (a decision made, a budget defended, hours back). Never the verb we perform ("plan", "track", "manage", "integrate").
- **Features only appear as the reason the outcome is believable** ("…because every deal is linked to the campaign that sourced it").
- **No outcome is claimed as delivered.** North has no customer results yet. Outcomes are written as what the reader *can do* with North, and each has a measure to baseline in pilots (table in section 7) so real results can replace them later.

## 1. Home

**Hero headline:** Show what marketing delivered, in revenue.
**Sub-headline:** North ties your plan, your spend and your deals together, so you can defend your budget with numbers finance accepts and put money where it works.
**Buttons:** Request a demo `[Stef: destination?]` · Watch the 2-minute overview · Sign in

**Five outcomes** (the core of the page; one line each, a short proof line underneath)
1. **Defend your budget with evidence, not adjectives.** Every dollar traces from plan to campaign to closed deal. *Because spend, activity and deals live in one chain.*
2. **Know which campaigns paid for themselves.** See the revenue each campaign sourced and what it cost. *Because deals link to the campaign that sourced them.*
3. **Be believed in the room.** Finance, sales and the board read the same numbers. *Because plan, report and board pack come from one source.*
4. **Spot a miss while you can still fix it.** Plan versus actual and the gap to target, all year. *Because the plan stays live beside the spend and pipeline.*
5. **Get your time back.** No more rebuilding the quarterly report by hand. *Because the report is the system, not a deck made from five spreadsheets.*

**The problem (short):** Most marketing reports are full of numbers finance can't use — impressions, clicks, engagement — so when someone asks "what did we get for that budget?", the answer is a story, not a figure. Only about half of senior marketing leaders (52%) say they can prove marketing's value and get credit for it (Gartner, 2024).

**The headline measure:** *Contribution to closed pipeline* — the closed-won revenue that traces to marketing, the share of all closed revenue that represents, and the return on the spend behind it, with the unattributed remainder shown, not hidden. `[VERIFY: org-wide tile specified, not built — see KPI spec; keep as "what North is built to measure" until built]`

**Trust strip (no logos yet):** your data stays separate from every other organisation's (enforced in the database); you decide who sees what; AI drafts, people approve.

## 4. What North is not
- **Not a CRM.** Your CRM stays your record of contacts and deals; North adds the plan-to-revenue line.
- **Not a dashboard of vanity metrics.** Every headline number traces to a plan, a spend line or a deal.
- **Not an attribution black box.** The rule is visible; unattributed revenue is shown.
- **Not a project tool in disguise.** The point is the result, not the task list.
- **Not an ad platform or content generator.**
- **Not an autopilot.** AI drafts; people approve.
- **Not a long implementation.** Start with a brief or your current plan.

## 6. Results (what the page can honestly say today)
Same structure and sources as v2 §7, with outcome-led captions. Do not state North-delivered results until measured in pilots.

**The problem, in numbers**
- Only **52%** of senior marketing leaders say they succeed in proving marketing's value and getting credit; CFOs (40%) and CEOs (39%) are ranked most sceptical. — Gartner, 378 leaders, 2024
- **47%** of CMOs say marketing is seen as an expense, not an investment. — Gartner, 395 CMOs, 2024
- Only **27%** of CEOs and CFOs say their CMO exceeded expectations. — Gartner, 125 CEOs/CFOs, 2024–25
- Marketing budgets average **7.8% of revenue**, and **56%** of CMOs say their budget doesn't cover their 2026 strategy. — Gartner 2026 CMO Spend Survey, 401 leaders (mostly large enterprises, NA/UK/Europe)
- **80%** of Australian CMOs say demonstrating ROI is their top priority. — LinkedIn B2B ROI research via Marketing-Interactive, Mar 2025 (method not published in the article)
- Only **30%** of B2B tech CMOs measure customer acquisition cost; **47%** say lack of time is their main barrier to analysis. — The CMO Survey 2025 via IT Brief Australia (sample not stated)

**What good practice is associated with** (associations, not guarantees)
- **62%** of leaders who meet regularly with their analytics team say they can prove value, vs **30%** of those who rarely do. — Gartner, 2024
- Teams that share buyer-journey insights are **1.6×** more likely to exceed revenue growth expectations. — Gartner B2B Commercial Strategy Survey, 412 leaders, 2023–24
