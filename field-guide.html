North — overview

North is nine separate tools ("modules"), each its own page, sharing one sign-in and one database. Which modules you can see and edit is controlled by three layers: your organization sets the ceiling (which modules exist at all), your role sets Configure/Publish/Can-edit, and you personally have a per-user Modules toggle. Every module shares the same top nav, the same small ? Help button, and the same red 💬 feedback button, so once you know one, the others feel familiar.

Strategy

Funnel planning, forecasting and the budget drivers every other module's numbers roll up from. Pages run roughly in workflow order: Drivers & rates → Planning engine → Geography & pods → Segment & audience → Activity plan → Campaign & cost, then Actuals & history → Insight → Snapshots.

How to

  1. Set your drivers and rates first. Confirm the conversion rates, costs and assumptions your forecast runs on — everything downstream reads these.
  2. Build the plan through Planning engine, Geography & pods and Segment & audience.
  3. Lay out activity and cost in Activity plan and Campaign & cost.
  4. Track actuals in Actuals & history so Insight can compare plan vs. actual.
  5. Snapshot the plan before a big revision, or to compare quarter over quarter.
  6. Use the quick calculator from the home screen for a fast what-if without touching the full plan.

The old Board report still exists at a direct link for continuity, but board-level reporting now lives in Reporting.

🧭 Worked example — clearing a blocking validation

"Why won't my funnel numbers add up?"

Strategy's home screen shows a live validations count next to your name (e.g. "7 blocking validations need your attention") — Ordo will not let downstream Campaign & cost numbers be trusted until these clear. Here's a real one, straight from this org's own list:

  1. Read the validation line. "Blocking — Segment mix totals 200%, not 100%." That's telling you Segment & audience's split across segments (e.g. Enterprise / Mid-market / SMB) sums to double what it should.
  2. Open Segment & audience from the Plan section of the nav.
  3. Find the segment whose percentages don't sum to 100. A 200% total usually means a segment got entered twice, or a percentage was typed as "50" in two rows that should have summed with others to 100 total, not 100 each.
  4. Correct the split so the segment's percentages sum to exactly 100%, then save.
  5. Recheck the home screen. The blocking count drops by one, and that segment's line disappears from the Validations list.

A similar pattern applies to the pod-share validations you'll also see here (e.g. "Japan pod shares total 20%", "ANZ pod shares total 70%") — those live on Geography & pods instead, but the fix is the same shape: find the region, make its pod shares sum to 100%, save.

FAQ

How do I reset my password?

An admin can trigger a reset from Configuration → Roles & users → Reset password, or use "Forgot password?" at login. The email comes from Supabase directly, not from North.

How do I set up a passkey?

From your account menu, choose to set up a passkey — your browser or device (Windows Hello, Touch ID, a security key) walks you through it.

Why can't I see a module, or can't edit something?

Access is layered: organization → role → your personal Modules toggle. Ask an admin to check all three.

What is Super Admin?

Not a role in the Roles table — a separate flag on a login that gives full cross-organization visibility in Configuration, independent of any one organization's roles.

Campaign Planning

Individual campaigns and their execution detail — budgets, dates, partners, templates, and how a campaign actually performed. This is where a campaign is born before it's pushed through to Strategy's funnel.

How to

  1. Start a new campaign. Click New campaign and pick one of four ways in: Fill the form (go straight to the full form), Answer a few questions (one field at a time), Upload a document (drop a brief — AI pulls what it can, or falls back to a basic prefill if no AI provider is configured), or Speak / dictate (works best in Chrome or Edge). All four land you in the normal form to review before anything's created.
  2. Choose Simple or Expert mode. Simple shows the core fields; Expert adds business case, sign-off and partner allocation. Simple-mode users only see Home and Planning in the side nav.
  3. Track effectiveness once a campaign has run.
  4. Manage partners and templates so recurring structures don't get rebuilt from scratch each time.
  5. Push to Strategy. New campaigns start local to Cursus — use Sync to push them through to Ordo when ready (a toast confirms it).

🧭 Worked example — standing up "FY27 Enterprise Field Series"

Taking a $180k field-marketing campaign from idea to sign-off queue

This is a real campaign sitting in this org's list today — Draft status, $180k budget. Here's the path it would have taken:

  1. New campaign → Fill the form (or Upload a document, if there's already a brief to hand — AI pulls the name, dates and budget out for you to check).
  2. Name it "FY27 Enterprise Field Series" and set the budget to $180,000.
  3. Pick Simple mode if this is a quick standard field campaign, or switch to Expert if it needs a business case and formal sign-off routing — a Field programme at this size usually wants Expert, for the sign-off trail alone.
  4. Save as Draft while the details are still being worked out — it won't show up as "awaiting sign-off" until it's actually submitted.
  5. When ready, submit for sign-off. Status moves to Submitted, and it now counts toward Strategy's "awaiting sign-off" tile.
  6. Sync it to Strategy once approved, so its budget rolls into the funnel's Campaign & cost numbers.

Compare against "RLS TEST CAMPAIGN" in the same list — Approved, $1 budget — a deliberately tiny fixture used for permissions testing, not a real campaign. If you ever see a $1 or $0 "campaign" in a real list, that's usually exactly this: a test row, not a mistake.

FAQ

What are the different ways to start a new campaign?

Fill the form, Answer a few questions, Upload a document, or Speak / dictate — all four land you in the same form to review before anything is created.

Why can't I use Speak / dictate?

It uses your browser's own speech recognition, which works best in Chrome or Edge — Safari's support is inconsistent. Upload a document or Fill the form work everywhere.

Is AI document/dictation reading always available?

If your organization hasn't configured an AI provider yet (Configuration → LLM providers), upload and dictation still work — they fall back to a basic prefill (name, first two dates, a dollar amount) instead of full extraction.

What's the difference between Simple and Expert mode?

Simple mode shows the core fields; Expert mode adds business case, sign-off, partner allocation and more — toggle it if your role allows.

Events

Event planning and logistics, rolling individual events up into programme-level views.

How to

  1. Log a new event from the Events list, then fill in its logistics in the event's own detail view.
  2. Check programme roll-ups to see totals across every event in a programme, rather than one at a time.
  3. Shared config (regions, cost buckets) reflects Configuration's settings — it isn't edited here.

🧭 Worked example — closing out an event

Marking "TEst event" Completed

A real event in this org: two-day run (28→29 July), $280k budget, now marked Completed. The general path for wrapping any event up:

  1. Open the event from the Events list once its dates have passed.
  2. Log final logistics in its detail view — actual attendance, final spend against the $280k budget, any outstanding vendor items.
  3. Set status to Completed. This is what should move it out of "upcoming" and into your historical record.
  4. Check the programme roll-up if this event belongs to a programme, so the programme-level totals pick up its final numbers.

Assets

The asset register — creative, collateral and merchandise tracked through their lifecycle, with a stock-transactions ledger underneath.

How to

  1. Browse or add assets. Each has a detail view for notes, expiry/publish dates, sign-off and a file link.
  2. Log transactions to track usage, reuse or handoffs.
  3. Review effectiveness to see how an asset actually performed.
  4. Check reference tracking for where an asset is referenced elsewhere in North.

🧭 Worked example — restocking a low-stock item

"Honey Jar branded gift sets" just hit 8 units

A real asset in this org, tagged Merchandise, currently sitting at 8 units with a $23 cost per unit — exactly the kind of thing the Low-stock tile is meant to catch.

  1. Open the asset from the Assets list — its detail view shows notes, expiry/publish dates, sign-off status and a file link (e.g. a spec sheet or supplier quote).
  2. Log a transaction for the restock — record the quantity received and, if relevant, which campaign or event drew the stock down in the first place.
  3. Tag it if it isn't already — this org's preset list includes Merchandise, Giveaway, Signage, Booth, AV equipment, Printed collateral, Badge/lanyard and a few others; pick from the presets rather than typing a new free-text tag where one already fits.
  4. Set (or check) the low-stock threshold on the asset so it keeps surfacing here at the right point in future, not too early or too late.

Note: the Low-stock tile on the home dashboard is currently affected by the duplicate-entries bug below — "Honey Jar branded gift sets" may show up twice in that list even though there's only one real record. The Assets table itself (and the transaction log) is not affected.

Targets

ICP (ideal customer profile) filtering and opportunity-level reporting.

How to

  1. Set your filters — this defines the ICP criteria you're targeting (industry, what you sell, ideal client description, deal value, market/country today).
  2. Review the target list of accounts/prospects matching your current filters.
  3. Run reports for opportunity-level detail off the target list.
  4. Compare multiple filter sets or target lists side by side.

🧭 Worked example — defining an ICP filter

Building a filter for a new vertical
  1. Open Filters and start a new one.
  2. Fill in the firmographic fields available today: industry (e.g. "Fintech"), what you sell (e.g. "Payments infrastructure"), an ideal-client description in plain language, an average deal value, and market/country (e.g. "AU/NZ").
  3. Save it — it immediately populates the Target list with whatever accounts/prospects match.
  4. Run a report off that list to see opportunity-level detail, or open Compare to weigh it against a second filter set (say, a different vertical) side by side.

Note: company size, seniority and job-title filters aren't in the current field set — if you need to narrow by those, do it downstream in Scoring or when pulling the list into Hub's own Network filters, which do support them today.

Scoring

A deliberately transparent deal-scoring calculator — weighted factors and a Hot/Warm/Cold threshold system, not a predictive model. Nothing here is "divined": every score traces back to a rule you can see.

How to

  1. Review deals on the working pipeline view.
  2. Check or tune the scoring model — this is where the weighted rules behind every score live.
  3. Look at Forecast for a scoring-driven view of what's likely to close.
  4. Browse Contacts for the people tied to your deals.

🧭 Worked example — reading a deal's score

Why a deal sits in a given tier

Every deal here is scored by summing a set of weighted factors your org has defined — nothing is inferred or predicted. To understand why a specific deal (say, one sitting in "Prospecting" with tier "—") hasn't been tiered yet:

  1. Open the deal from the Deals list.
  2. Check the Scoring model to see which factors feed the total — a deal with no tier yet usually just hasn't had its factor inputs filled in (deal size, engagement level, fit criteria — whatever your org's model weighs).
  3. Fill in the missing inputs on the deal itself; the score and tier (Hot/Warm/Cold) recompute immediately.
  4. Check Forecast to see how that tier feeds into the likely-to-close view.

Because every factor and threshold is visible in the Scoring model, you can always trace a Hot/Warm/Cold label back to the exact numbers that produced it — that transparency is a deliberate design choice, not a limitation.

⚠️

Known: Scoring's own deal count currently disagrees with what the Dashboard and Reporting say about it — see Known issues.

Process Maps

Process maps and workflow automation — diagrams plus rules that fire alerts when real conditions in North's data are met.

How to

  1. Browse or build a diagram from the Diagrams list, opening one into the editor to draw or edit it.
  2. Set up automations — rules that trigger on real events (campaign ready-to-execute, off-target, task overdue, deal stage, event status, asset low-stock) and fire an email/chat alert to a person or "the manager" of whoever owns the matched record.
  3. Start from a template where one's offered, rather than building an automation from scratch.

Automations are evaluated client-side while someone has this page open (checked on load and every 5 minutes) — there's no background server firing them the instant a condition becomes true.

🧭 Worked example — alert the manager when a campaign goes off target

Building "campaign_off_target" → notify the manager
  1. Go to Automations and start a new rule (or copy the template, if one's offered).
  2. Pick the trigger type "campaign off target" — this mirrors Strategy/Campaign Planning's own "At risk" RAG check (committed or actual cost meaningfully over plan).
  3. Set the threshold if you want a different tolerance than the org default — e.g. 100% (fire only once it's fully over budget) vs. a tighter 90% early-warning.
  4. Set the target to "the manager." This resolves per matched campaign, not per rule — Schema walks Campaign owner → their business unit → that unit's manager, so two campaigns with different owners correctly notify two different people.
  5. Choose the alert channel — email or chat.
  6. Save, then leave this tab open occasionally (or reopen it every so often) — remember automations only evaluate while someone has this page open, on load and every 5 minutes, since there's no background job firing them.

The moment a campaign crosses the threshold you set, its owner's manager gets a one-time alert (deduped, so it won't refire for the same breach) — no code change needed to add a rule like this for a different campaign or a different threshold.

⚠️

Known: this was the slowest North module to load in testing (~10s before it settled) — not broken, just worth expecting a short wait.

Configuration

Shared configuration — the admin-facing module every other module pulls its settings from. Most pages are read-only unless your role has Configure access.

How to — the basics

  1. Manage roles & users. This is also where you generate an invite code: pick a role, click Generate invite code, send it to your teammate — they redeem it at signup. An admin can trigger a password reset from here too.
  2. Set cost buckets, tracking IDs and campaign naming — the shared taxonomies every module's campaigns/activities draw from.
  3. Set currency for how money displays and calculates across every module.
  4. Set up integrations and check Agent activity for automated/agent-driven activity history.
  5. Review Feedback — issues reported from the 💬 button anywhere in North land here with name, page and timestamp captured automatically.

🧭 Worked example — bringing on a new teammate

Inviting someone as a Manager
  1. Go to Roles & users and click Generate invite code.
  2. Pick the role — e.g. "Manager" — before generating. The code is tied to that role; you can't change it after redemption without re-assigning the role manually.
  3. Copy the generated code (an 8-character code, e.g. DA132163) and send it to your new teammate however you'd normally reach them — email, Slack, whatever's easiest. North doesn't email it for you.
  4. They redeem it at North's sign-up screen, choosing "I have an invite code," entering the code plus their own name and password.
  5. Check Roles & users afterward — the invite table shows who redeemed each code, so you can confirm it landed with the right person.

If you can see their row afterward but the fields look greyed out with a 🔒, that just means their role sits at or above your own tier — you can view but not edit them unless you're Super Admin or it's your own row. That's the access model working as designed, not a bug.

How to — LLM providers

  1. Add a provider on the LLM providers page — Anthropic, OpenAI or Gemini. Configuring one unlocks the richer AI extraction (document upload, dictation, Hub's content-generation buttons) for every signed-in user in your organization, not just admins.
  2. Set the Access type on each provider: api (North/Hub can call it programmatically — this is the only kind that actually powers AI features), desktop (a desktop app install someone uses manually) or web (a browser-based tool someone uses manually). Only api-type providers show up as assignable options elsewhere — recording a desktop or web tool here is just inventory, not something North can call.
  3. Mark one provider Active (the checkbox) to set the organization-wide default every AI feature falls back to when a task has no provider of its own assigned.

How to — Agent Registry

A catalog of every agent, skill and scheduled task registered anywhere in North or Hub/Queue for your organization — what it does, which AI runs it (if any), how often, and whether it's active. North is the master for this catalog: add, edit, activate or remove an entry from here, or from AH's Settings → Library — both write to the same place, but anything shared with North can only be changed from here.

  1. Add or edit an entry from Configuration → Agent Registry (full add/edit/delete access). Give it a name, kind, and mark Used in — one entry can be used in North, Hub, and/or Queue at once, so check every surface it actually affects.
  2. Assign an AI provider to a specific task (rather than following the org-wide default) by editing that entry's AI provider field — for example, to move "Dashboard summary (Ask Alec)" off Gemini onto Anthropic: open the entry, pick the provider from the dropdown, save. It takes effect on the very next use, no code change.
  3. Understand the AH side. AH's Settings → Library only shows entries used in Hub or Queue — an entry used exclusively in North never appears there. An entry shared with North shows in AH as read-only (🔒 Edit in North) — Hub users can see it but changes have to come from here.

🧭 Worked example — moving "Dashboard summary" off Gemini

Switching one AI feature to a different provider, step by step

This is a real, previously-answered question from this org — here's the exact path:

  1. Configure the provider first, if it isn't already. Go to Configuration → LLM providers → add a row for the provider you want (say, Anthropic). Set its Access type to API — anything else won't show up as an assignable option in the next step.
  2. Open the Agent Registry — either here in Configuration, or AH's Settings → Library works identically since both write to the same catalog.
  3. Find and edit "Dashboard summary (Ask Alec)."
  4. Open the AI provider dropdown — it now lists every api-type provider your org has configured. Pick Anthropic.
  5. Save. No code change, no deploy — it takes effect the very next time anyone clicks Ask Alec for a dashboard summary.

To change the org-wide default instead of just this one task, go back to LLM providers and tick Active on the provider row you want as the fallback — leave a task's own AI provider field blank to have it follow whatever's marked Active, rather than pinning it to one provider permanently.

FAQ

How do I invite a new teammate?

Configuration → Roles & users → Generate invite code, after picking their role. They redeem it at signup by choosing "I have an invite code."

What does Cross-region visibility mean?

"Transparent" means you see every region in your organization. "Isolated" means you only see the regions specifically checked for you.

What does the Super Admin cross-org view bar do?

Lets a Super Admin temporarily view another organization's data across the modules it's wired into, without switching accounts.

How do I get to AH or AQ from North?

Click the AH tile on North's home dashboard ("Open →") — it carries your session across automatically. Going to AH by typing its URL directly does not carry a session across; always go by way of North's own link.

I'm in Hub — how do Contact records and campaigns relate to North?

Hub and North share one pool of contact/outreach data per Hub login. A contact sourced in Hub becomes a real Hub contact immediately; outreach/campaign activity in Hub is a separate thing from North's own Campaign Planning (Cursus) campaigns, even though both use the word "campaign."

Can an Admin move or copy a Hub user's data to another account?

Hub's Settings → Users → Reassign data supports Move (takes data off the source account) and, for accounts with the Duplicate capability, Copy (leaves the source untouched).

Why can't I edit an Agent Registry entry from AH's Library?

Because it's also used in North — North is the master for anything shared with it, so AH shows it read-only with a link back here. Entries used only in Hub or Queue stay fully editable from AH.

AH & AQ — overview

Account Intelligence Hub (AH) is the contact-seeker and outreach workspace — companies, contacts, signals, outreach, campaigns and reporting. Account Queue (AQ) is a separate, focused page for working through LinkedIn connection requests one profile at a time. Both share one login and one pool of contact/outreach data; the top nav (🗂 AH · 🔍 AQ · 🧭 North · ❓ Help) switches between all three without signing in again. North holds no identifiable people by design — contact records live only in Hub.

Account Intelligence Hub

Reached from North's home dashboard (the AH tile) or a direct AH link once already signed in. The sidebar covers Dashboard, Network, Outreach, Campaigns, Signals, Reporting and Searches, plus Cloud Sync and Settings under Account.

How to — find, add and enrich contacts

  1. Search. Network → Find & Enrich, or the 🔎 Searches chip, runs a LinkedIn people search in the background (~10–15 min) — de-duplicated against your existing contacts. Check back via Searches.
  2. Add a contact manually from Network → + Add contact — name is the only required field; everything else (company, title, warmth, LinkedIn URL/connection degree, source, pipeline stage, tags) is optional at creation.
  3. Filter the contact list by stage, connection degree, campaign, reply status, queue status, DNC/Not Found flags, warmth, source or follow-up status — Network's filter bar covers all of these at once.
  4. Enrich a contact or company via configured providers (Google CSE, PDL, Apollo, Lusha, FindyMail, BetterContact, Hunter.io, ZoomInfo) — add each provider's API key under Settings first; Hub prompts you if a key a feature needs is missing.

🧭 Worked example — a real follow-up, start to finish

Working "Mark Brixton @ Quantaco" off the Needs-attention list

A real contact sitting in this org's "⚠️ Needs attention — no contact in 7+ days" list today, with a follow-up due 2026-09-21:

  1. Open Network → All Contacts and search "Mark Brixton," or click straight through from the Dashboard's Needs-attention card.
  2. Check the contact panel's Signals tab for Quantaco first — anything logged there (a funding round, a new hire) gives you a real reason to reach out, rather than a cold "just checking in."
  3. Log the outreach once you've reached out — pick a channel (LinkedIn/Email/Both) and status (Contacted is the right starting point).
  4. Set the next follow-up using the +7/+14/+30-day shortcuts, or clear it if the thread's now closed.
  5. Add a note in the Notes field — it auto-saves on blur, so there's no separate Save button to remember.

How to — campaigns

  1. Create a campaign from Campaigns → Create a campaign: name, Segment (AU / APAC / Offshore-APAC), start date, daily volume, Status (Planning/Running/Paused/Completed/Archived), per-tier contact targets (Tier 1 C-suite/MD/VP/GP, Tier 2 Director/Partner/RVP, Tier 3 Sr Mgr/Head of/NED), and free-text targeting notes/ICP.
  2. Leave a new campaign on Planning until you're ready — Running is what allows sourcing/sending against it.
  3. Track performance per campaign (connection rate, reply rate) from the Campaigns list or the Dashboard's Campaign performance panel.

🧭 Worked example — exactly what was done to build a safe test campaign

Creating "ZZ-TEST-1409-DO-NOT-USE"

This isn't hypothetical — it's the real campaign created in this org during today's UAT pass, as a sandbox to test against without touching live data:

  1. Campaigns → Create a campaign.
  2. Campaign name: ZZ-TEST-1409-DO-NOT-USE — the ZZ- prefix sorts it to the bottom of any alphabetical list, and the name itself tells the next person not to source or send against it.
  3. Segment: left on the default, AU.
  4. Status: Planning — deliberately, since Planning campaigns can't be sourced or sent against, so a test campaign never accidentally goes live.
  5. Daily volume, dates, tier targets: left blank — none of them were needed for what this campaign was for.
  6. Targeting notes / ICP: a plain-language note explaining who made it, when, and why — "Created by the '1409 - Testing' live UAT skill (2026-09-14) as a safe sandbox campaign. Do not source/send against this — delete once testing is done."
  7. Save. It immediately appeared in the Campaigns list and in Reporting's campaign picker, exactly like any real campaign would.

This pattern — a clearly-prefixed name, Planning status, and a note explaining its purpose — is the safe way to create any throwaway/test record in AH without it being mistaken for real pipeline.

How to — outreach, signals & reporting

  1. Log outreach against a contact from their detail view — status values include Contacted, Connection Pending, Replied and Meeting.
  2. Log a Company Signal (Hiring, New to Role, Funding, New Win, Expansion, Engaged on LI, New hire, Other) from a company's own Signals tab — the Signals dashboard then rolls these up: total, new this week, companies with any signal, and companies "gone quiet" 30+ days.
  3. Build a report on Reporting — pick a period basis (outreach activity or contact added), a range, granularity, and optionally a campaign to compare against another.

How to — settings & administration

  1. Add enrichment provider API keys under Settings — each provider has its own key field.
  2. Manage your Agent Registry entries under Settings → Library — see Configuration for the North-is-master rule that governs what's editable here.
  3. Reassign a user's data via Settings → Users → Reassign data (Move, or Copy if the account holds the Duplicate capability).
  4. Back up to the cloud via Cloud Sync — contacts, outreach, templates, companies and campaigns to a SQLite store via a local proxy; the status badge shows connected/offline. Also gated to org admins, and pulls campaigns in from North.

FAQ

Are AH and North the same data?

They share the same underlying pool of contacts/outreach per your Hub login, but they're separate tools. A contact sourced in AH becomes a real contact right away; outreach/campaign activity in AH isn't the same thing as a North Campaign Planning campaign, even though both use the word "campaign."

What are the user roles in AH?

A separate five-tier ladder from North's roles: user < planner < manager < admin < super_user, per organization — AH has no "all organizations" concept the way North's Super Admin flag does.

Where do I get help inside AH?

The ❓ Help link opens North's Configuration Help panel in a new tab, with real AH-specific questions included (like this one) — AH doesn't have a separate in-app panel of its own.

Do I need an API key to use AH?

Not to use AH itself — but a specific enrichment feature (Google CSE, PDL, Apollo, Lusha, FindyMail, BetterContact, Hunter.io, ZoomInfo) needs its own key added under Settings first. AH tells you which key is missing when you hit a feature that needs one.

Why did "Sync now" used to time out, and is it fixed?

With several campaigns, sync used to run one at a time sequentially and could exceed the timeout. It now runs campaigns in parallel — fixed.

Account Queue

A focused, one-profile-at-a-time tool for LinkedIn connection requests. Its own description says it plainly: "Prep mode — open profiles, review, then click Connect yourself. Never send in bulk." Contacts are grouped into weeks and, inside each week, day-batches.

How to

  1. Open a week and a day-batch. A completed batch shows a ✅ "batch complete" state.
  2. Work the queue card by card. Connect is always manual — AQ opens the profile for you to review and you click Connect yourself on LinkedIn; AQ then logs it in AH as "Connection Pending" automatically. Skip → moves past a contact without an outcome; Next → marks the card done.
  3. Use the Connection Note tab for a draft message (300-character limit, matching LinkedIn's own cap).
  4. Archive a finished week with 🗄, and restore one with ↩ if you need it back.

🧭 Worked example — working a real batch

Wave 1 · Mktg, 46/50 done

A real, in-progress wave from this org — its Tuesday day-tab had 15 contacts, of which 13 were marked done and 2 skipped, leaving a "✅ batch complete" state on that day. Contacts in the queue included Leandro Perez (SVP & CMO, Salesforce), Renee Amor (CMO, Nuix) and Jeremy Cooper (VP Marketing, Databricks). The flow for a fresh card in a batch like this:

  1. Open the day-tab (e.g. "tue (15)") to load its stack of cards.
  2. Review the profile AQ opens for you — check title, company and any note already attached before deciding.
  3. Decide: Connect, Skip, or Next. If you're going to connect, add a short note on the Connection Note tab first (300 characters, same as LinkedIn's own limit) — then go to LinkedIn yourself and click Connect there. AQ never does this step for you.
  4. Come back and mark the card done (or Skip → if you're passing on this one for now) — AQ logs a manual Connect as "Connection Pending" in AH automatically once you confirm it.
  5. Move to the next card until the day-tab shows "✅ batch complete."

How to — Warm-Up

  1. Manual Warm-Up is you actively working through the queue right now.
  2. Auto Warm-Up automates the low-risk part only — viewing and following, never connecting. Open its Settings, pick active days (Mon–Fri) and which day-tabs feed the eligible pool, then Start. It runs unattended on a scheduled task (Sydney time) — the tab doesn't need to stay open.
  3. Each Auto Warm-Up run independently picks Look or Follow (not a fixed alternating pattern); a "Looked" contact gets revisited 1–3 days later to Follow. It never sends a Connect request — that stays with you.
  4. Stop Auto Warm-Up any time from the same settings panel; its recent actions are logged there.

FAQ

Does AQ ever send a connection request automatically?

No. Connect is always a manual, one-at-a-time action. Auto Warm-Up automates viewing and following only.

What's the difference between a "week" and a "batch"?

A week is the larger grouping (roughly a week's worth of contacts); a batch is a day-tab inside it. You work through one batch at a time as a stack of cards.

What does 1st/2nd/3rd degree mean, and does it stick?

How connected you already are to that person on LinkedIn. Marking a degree syncs to the server immediately and should persist across devices/reloads — if one you set ever reverts, that's worth reporting via 💬, it shouldn't happen.
