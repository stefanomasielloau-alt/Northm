North's job is not only to connect the many systems marketing runs on, but to give marketing credibility — a plan, the spend, the activity and the closed revenue in one chain that finance, sales, the board, partners and buyers can all read.

## 1. Home

**Hero headline:** Marketing, proven.
**Sub-headline:** North connects your plan, your campaigns, your spend and your closed pipeline — so marketing can show what it contributed, in numbers finance recognises.
**Buttons:** Request a demo `[Stef: destination?]` · Watch the 2-minute overview `[video — see video plan]` · Sign in

**The problem (lead with this, it's the emotional hook)**
Most marketing reports are full of numbers nobody in finance can use: impressions, clicks, "engagement". They rarely connect to a deal, so when the CFO asks "what did we get for that budget?", the answer is a story, not a figure. That is why marketing keeps being treated as a cost to cut instead of an investment to back.

Industry research says the same thing (sources below): only about half of senior marketing leaders say they can prove marketing's value and get credit for it (Gartner, 2024), and in a separate Gartner survey nearly half of CMOs said marketing is viewed as an expense rather than a strategic investment.

**What changes with North** (3 beats)
1. **One chain, not five documents.** Strategy → campaigns → activities → spend → deals → closed revenue, all in one system, so any number can be traced back to what produced it.
2. **A credible headline number.** *Contribution to closed pipeline*: the value of closed-won deals that trace to marketing campaigns, the share of all closed-won that represents, and the return on the spend behind it — with the unattributed remainder shown, not hidden. `[VERIFY: org-wide headline tile is specified, not built — see KPI spec]`
3. **Everyone sees the same thing.** Finance, sales, the board and partners read the same plan and the same figures instead of each getting a different deck.

**How it works** (numbered, from v1, tightened)
1. Set the plan — targets by region, stream and segment.
2. Plan the campaigns and budgets that deliver it — down to cost lines.
3. Run events and manage assets against the same campaigns.
4. Link deals to campaigns, score and forecast.
5. Report up: contribution to closed pipeline, plan vs actual, board report.

**Trust strip (no logos yet — security and control instead)**
Each organisation's data is separate (enforced in the database); roles control who can view, edit, publish and configure; AI drafts, people approve.

**Closing CTA:** Show your board what marketing delivered. → Request a demo

## 2. Why North — "marketing that proves its value"

**Headline:** Vanity numbers don't survive a budget meeting.

Write this page as short paragraphs plus the stat cards from "Results" below. Suggested flow:
- *The experience:* the quarterly review where marketing presents reach and engagement and the CFO asks about revenue. 
- *Why it happens:* the plan lives in a deck, spend in a spreadsheet, activity in a project tool, deals in the CRM, and nobody owns the line between them.
- *What credible looks like:* a fixed, visible attribution rule; the same numbers in the plan and the report; finance able to inspect the underlying deals; unattributed revenue shown honestly.
- *What North does about it:* the chain in section 1, plus the KPI.
- *The honest limit:* attribution is a method, not magic. North makes the method explicit and consistent so that the number is defensible — it does not claim to know exactly which touch "caused" a sale.

## 4. What North is not

Put this on the Product page and as a short strip on Home. It builds trust.

- **Not a CRM.** Your CRM stays your system of record for contacts and deals; North connects to it and adds the plan-to-revenue line. (Hub holds contacts and outreach beside the plan; it isn't a CRM replacement.)
- **Not another dashboard of vanity metrics.** Every headline number traces to a plan, a spend line or a deal.
- **Not an attribution black box.** The rule is visible and fixed; unattributed revenue is shown.
- **Not a project tool with a marketing skin.** Tasks exist, but the point is the plan, budget and result.
- **Not an ad platform or content generator.** It doesn't buy media or write your campaigns; AI helps with drafting and review inside the planning work.
- **Not an autopilot.** AI drafts; people approve. Nothing is created until you press Create.
- **Not a long implementation project.** Start with a brief or your current plan.

## 7. Results

**Honesty rule for this section:** North has no customer outcomes to report yet, so the page must not imply any. It shows (a) what the research says about the problem and about what good practice is associated with, each with its source, and (b) what North is built to make measurable. When you have pilot customers, add a third block — real, permissioned results — using the case-study template at the end.

**Block A — The problem, in numbers**
- **About half** of senior marketing leaders (52%) say they succeed in proving marketing's value and getting credit for it. CFOs (40%) and CEOs (39%) are ranked as the most sceptical executives. — *Gartner, survey of 378 senior marketing leaders, Apr–May 2024; press release 18 Sep 2024*
- **47%** of CMOs say marketing is viewed as an expense rather than a strategic investment. — *Gartner CMO survey (395 CMOs, Feb–Mar 2024), same release*
- **27%** of CEOs and CFOs say their CMO exceeded expectations last year; **22%** say they've had significant clarity from their CMO on marketing accountabilities. — *Gartner, 125 CEOs and CFOs, Aug–Sep 2024; release Feb 2025*
- **56%** of CMOs say their budget doesn't cover their 2026 strategy; marketing budgets average **7.8% of company revenue**. — *Gartner 2026 CMO Spend Survey, 401 marketing leaders, mostly large enterprises in North America, UK and Europe; May 2026*
- **78%** of B2B CMOs say proving campaign ROI has grown in importance over two years; **80%** of Australian CMOs say demonstrating ROI is their top priority; nearly half face monthly budget justification. — *LinkedIn B2B ROI research as reported by Marketing-Interactive, Mar 2025 (respondent count and method not published in the article)*
- Only **30%** of B2B tech CMOs measure customer acquisition cost and **50%** assess campaign-level ROI; **47%** say lack of time is the main barrier to performance analysis. — *The CMO Survey 2025 (Touchdown PR / Freeform Dynamics / Radma Research) as reported by IT Brief Australia, 5 Nov 2025 (sample size not stated)*
- Marketers used about **42%** of their martech stack's capabilities, down from 58% in 2020. — *Gartner, 324 marketers, 2022 — older; use only with the year shown*

**Block B — What good practice is associated with** (associations, not guarantees)
- Leaders who regularly meet their marketing analytics team: **62%** say they can prove value vs **30%** of those who meet infrequently. Leaders using two or more complex metric types were up to **1.8×** more likely to prove value than those using none. — *Gartner, Sep 2024 (same release as above)*
- Marketing and sales collaborate on only **3 of 15** commercial activities; teams that share buyer-journey insights are **1.6×** more likely to exceed revenue growth expectations. — *Gartner B2B Commercial Strategy Survey, 412 senior marketing and sales leaders, Nov–Dec 2023; release Jun 2024*

**Block C — What North is built to make measurable** (the bridge; these are capabilities, not outcomes)
- Spend that adds up from cost line to campaign to plan.
- Closed-won deals traced to the campaigns that sourced them, with cost per outcome.
- Contribution to closed pipeline as a share of all closed revenue and a return on spend, with unattributed revenue shown.
- One set of numbers from plan to board report.

**Block D — Real results (empty until you have pilots).** Case-study template: baseline before North (hours spent on quarterly reporting; share of closed-won linked to a campaign; budget variance) → measure after 90 days → permissioned quote. Don't publish a number without a measured baseline.

**Do not use** (could not be verified at the source, or too old/conflicted): "21% of marketers can measure revenue contribution" (2017, from a competing vendor's own survey); "only 22% feel sure they can justify spend to the CFO" and "66% lack confidence in revenue goals" (found via secondary sites; not opened at the primary source).
